From Form to Follow-Up: Automate Lead Capture with n8n

Nov 23, 2026 · 8 min read

Your form collects leads. Your follow-up doesn't. The gap between "someone subscribed" and "you said hello" is where conversions die. Here's the n8n chain that closes it, node for node.

The problem: slow follow-up costs you the lead

You run a Google Form on your site. A visitor fills it in at 9:00 a.m. By 5:00 p.m., you've "looked at it" — but it's already two hours old. The prospect moved on. A survey by HubSpot found that 80% of sales require five follow-ups, yet most teams reply over an hour after a lead lands.

The reason is structural, not a willpower problem: there is no automated first touch. Your brain is the only thing that sees the form submission. Form → spreadsheet → you → you → email = five minutes that nobody gets back.

The fix is a three-node pipeline that runs without you:

  • Capture the new form entry.
  • Route it: one row in your CRM/Sheets, plus a score or tag.
  • Notify immediately: welcome email + Slack/Telegram alert to the person who owns the lead.

Do that in under a second and you own the "first touch" window. Every extra hour you wait drops your reply rate by ~50% (and your conversion rate by even more).

Pattern 1: The real-time version (Webhook trigger)

For a live form on your site, the fastest path is a webhook. A small Google Apps Script forwards each form submission to your n8n webhook, and n8n does the rest. No polling, no missed rows.

The workflow, top to bottom:

  1. Webhook node receives the payload from Google Apps Script.
  2. Set node renames fields to your canonical schema (e.g. first_name, email, source). Form field names change; your downstream nodes don't.
  3. Google Sheets (overwrite / upsert) writes the row into your CRM sheet, by email, so re-submissions update instead of duplicating.
  4. Branch: is this a known lead or new?
  5. Brevo (or Mailchimp / ConvertKit) EmailSend sends the welcome email.
  6. Slack or Telegram webhook nudges the owner so the second touch is human, not automatic.

That's six nodes. The pattern is identical if you swap a specific tool (Brevo vs Mailchimp vs ConvertKit) — swap the final sender and keep the rest.

Node-by-node walkthrough

The only nodes you need, in order. I'll use the Slack alert as the example output, but substitute Telegram or Brevo/E-mail without changing the shape.

{
  "trigger": "Webhook",
  "name": "Form Webhook",
  "requestBody": {
    "mode": "capture",
    "path": "form-submit",
    "responseMode": "onReceived"
  }
}

Google Forms can't call an external endpoint directly, so add a tiny Google Apps Script on the form's side:

function onFormSubmit(e) {
  const form = FormApp.getActiveForm();
  const resp = e.response;
  const data = {};
  resp.getItemResponses().forEach(function(r) {
    data[r.getItem().getTitle()] = r.getResponse();
  });
  UrlFetchApp.fetch("https://your-n8n.vercel.app/webhook/form", {
    "method": "post",
    "contentType": "application/json",
    "payload": JSON.stringify({
      "first_name": data["Full Name"],
      "email": data["Email"],
      "source": "web",
      "submitted_at": new Date().toISOString()
    })
  });
}

Back in n8n, the Set node normalises your fields:

{
  "first_name": $json["Full Name"] || $json.first_name,
  "email": $json.Email || $json.email,
  "source": "web",
  "submitted_at": $json["Submitted At"]
}

Then the Google Sheets node — upsert on email so a returning lead updates their row:

// Google Sheets (overwrite)
// Spreadsheet: "leads"
// Column A: email (unique key)
// Upsert: TRUE
// Match column: Email
// Data: $json.first_name, $json.email, $json.source, $json.submitted_at

Finally the Slack webhook (or Telegram/Brevo):

{
  "text": "NEW LEAD  \nName: {{ $json.first_name }}  \nEmail: {{ $json.email }}  \nSource: {{ $json.source }}  \nReply link: {{ $json.reply_url }}"
}

Run it, hit your form once, and you'll see the Slack message arrive in seconds — while the lead is still fresh.

Where most people leak

The difference between a pipeline that runs and one that converts is in the edges. Two leaks kill this flow:

  • No capture of the source. If you never record where the lead came from (organic, paid, referral, direct), you can't tell which channel is worth your ad budget. Write source into the row on every write.
  • A welcome email that arrives one day later. Send the email immediately from the webhook, not from a daily digest. Delayed automation is no automation.

A third, quieter leak is a webhook that times out. Google Apps Script's UrlFetchApp.fetch defaults to a 10-second timeout. If n8n takes longer than that on the Sheets write, the script errors and the form's user gets a "submission failed" message. Set a generous timeout and handle errors on the n8n side with an Error Trigger workflow so nothing is silently dropped.

How to test without sending junk

Before you wire your live form to a real email provider, you want to prove the chain without spamming anyone:

  • Run the workflow once in test mode (n8n's "Listen for Test Event") and inspect the payload at each node.
  • Point the Slack webhook at your own test channel first. Verify the fields render correctly.
  • Point the Brevo node at "test mode" (most providers have a toggle) or use a Mock node in place of the sender.
  • Once it works, switch the sender to your real API key and send a test email to your own address.

You are looking for three things: does every field survive the Set node, does a second submit update the existing row (upsert) instead of duplicating, and does the welcome email land in the inbox (not spam) on the first real send.

The one mistake that kills this

It's treating the form submission as your product. The form is not your product; the follow-up is. A beautifully wired pipeline that sends a generic "Thanks for subscribing" email — with no next step, no human review, and no way to change tone — will not grow your business. The pipeline should hand the lead to a human after the automated first touch: the alert tells the owner the lead has arrived, the welcome email gives the first value, and the CRM row holds the conversation.

Quick checklist

  • Webhook path is stable and documented on the form side.
  • Set node exhausts every field into canonical names.
  • Google Sheets upsert is keyed on email — no duplicate rows.
  • Welcome email is the first thing sent, and it is personal enough to be useful.
  • A Slack/Telegram alert reaches the person who owns the lead.
  • An Error Trigger workflow alerts you to failed runs, not just successful ones.
